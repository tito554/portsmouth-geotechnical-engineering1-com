One of the costliest mistakes on a Portsmouth site involves underestimating how much loose backfill and buried chalk hollows can move once you start dewatering. Contractors along the M275 corridor and around Portsea Island often encounter running sands or soft organic silts directly above the White Chalk Group, and a standard compaction approach simply won’t stabilise the contact zone. Our grouting design service translates borehole data into a precise injection pattern—specifying grout type, set times, and target permeability—so you gain predictable Improvement before shoring or excavation begins. When the stratigraphy shows lenses of gravelly drift, we often pair the injection plan with a CPT investigation to refine the refusal criteria, and for deep basements near the historic dockyard we integrate the grout curtain with deep excavation monitoring to validate performance during each excavation stage.

A well-engineered grouting program in Portsmouth isn’t about pumping volume—it’s about matching rheology to the exact grain-size distribution of the harbour formation so you get penetration without fracture.

Process overview

Portsmouth’s coastal setting, with a tidal range exceeding 4.5 metres and a shallow water table across the reclaimed land of Gunwharf and the Naval Base, creates a working environment where grouting must perform in fully saturated, often saline conditions. Microfine cements and low-viscosity permeation grouts are typically selected to penetrate the silty fine sands of the Brickearth deposits without causing hydrofracture. Our designs follow BS EN 1997-1:2004 and BS 5930, incorporating limit-state verification for the treated ground mass. Key design parameters include target unconfined compressive strength of the grouted soil (commonly 1.5–3.0 MPa after 28 days), permeability reduction below 1×10⁻⁶ m/s, and grout take limits set by the void ratio of the natural formation. For sites where chalk dissolution pipes are suspected—a well-documented hazard on Portsea Island—the injection programme sequences compaction grouting to fill voids before proceeding with permeation treatment of the surrounding fractured chalk. The specification also defines real-time monitoring thresholds: injection pressure, flow rate, and surface heave, with automatic stop criteria if vertical movement exceeds 5 mm during any single pass.

Local context

Portsmouth sits at an average elevation of just 8 metres above sea level, with much of its commercial construction occurring on reclaimed land where the fill thickness often exceeds 3 metres. When grouting is omitted or poorly specified, differential settlement can appear within the first six months—particularly where untreated loose fill transitions abruptly into the dense chalk bedrock. A 2021 basement excavation near Southsea reported groundwater inflows exceeding 2 litres per second through untreated gravel lenses, causing a three-week programme delay while emergency permeation grouting was mobilised. The variable depth of the chalk surface, which can drop by several metres across a single building footprint due to buried solution features, means a desktop study alone cannot define the grout profile. Without exploratory drilling to map the rockhead, the risk of injecting into open fissures without containment is high. Our risk assessment includes a desk study of BGS mapping, targeted rotary drilling to confirm chalk quality, and a grout trial panel to validate the design before production works commence.

Quick answers

What grouting methods work best in Portsmouth’s loose harbour fill?

Permeation grouting with microfine cements is typically the first choice for the silty sands and gravelly drift found across Portsea Island. Where the fill contains larger voids or where the Brickearth is very loose, compaction grouting with a low-mobility mortar is used first to densify the mass, followed by permeation treatment to reduce hydraulic conductivity below 1×10⁻⁶ m/s. The method selection depends on the grain-size curve from site-specific boreholes, and we always validate the design with a trial panel before production.

How do you design grouting for chalk with dissolution features?

Chalk dissolution pipes are a recognised hazard in the Portsmouth area. Our approach uses rotary open-hole drilling to identify void locations and geometry, then sequences compaction grouting to fill the cavities with a stiff, low-slump grout. Once the major openings are sealed, a permeation phase treats the fractured chalk surrounding the feature. Post-treatment verification includes cored boreholes and water pressure testing at multiple depths to confirm the chalk mass meets the specified permeability target.

What is the typical cost for a grouting design package in Portsmouth?

A complete grouting design package for a Portsmouth site—including desk study, ground model development, grout specification, trial panel design, and verification protocol—ranges from £1,100 to £2,980, depending on the treated volume and complexity of the ground conditions. This is the design and specification service; the grouting contractor’s execution costs are separate and quoted based on the approved design.

Is a trial panel always required before full-scale grouting?

For most Portsmouth sites, yes. A trial panel is essential to confirm that the selected grout rheology and injection parameters actually achieve the target permeability and strength in the specific ground conditions. The trial validates refusal criteria, injection pressure limits, and grout take volumes. In variable ground like the harbour fill–chalk transition, skipping the trial panel introduces a significant risk of under-treatment or hydrofracture during production.
